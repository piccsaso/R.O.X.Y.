# Skinner Ridge project history

## Goal

Build a first-person Mars walk from Perseverance observations for the NASA hackathon. The demo starts near a layered slab in the Skinner Ridge workspace, lets the player walk past it and turn back, and keeps the player grounded using the same surface as the rendered terrain.

## 1. Site selection

- Compared Skinner Ridge at Hawksbill Gap, the Jezero western-wall overlook, the Sol 1709 rim drive, and Wildcat Ridge. Checked PDS mesh products, their labels and coordinate frames, and overlap on the ground.
- Located the Sol 1709 products through PDS Atlas Release 15 after a legacy archive route failed. NASA’s published drive reconstruction used 53 Navcam stereo pairs. The nine verified public Navcam mesh acquisitions at the examined station shared one rover body pose.
- Selected Skinner Ridge because nearby rover stops provided stronger measured overlap across a short walking lane. Used the Drive 1004 sample as a format and processing reference, then built a separate mesh from individual archive products.

[Site comparison, archive links, and overlap evidence](SITE_SELECTION.md)

## 2. First walkable build

- Fused eight stereo-derived Navcam observations from two rover stops into a 7.0 × 6.3 m patch. Used the PDS Site 26 coordinate frame, filtered disconnected fragments and stretched triangles, and exported an embedded-texture GLB with matching collision data.
- Built a local Three.js viewer with mouse look, WASD, a 1.65 m eye height, and reset.
- Verified a 3.29 m walk and look-back in Chrome. The test stopped at the crop edge and a direct approach stopped short of the slab. Cutouts remained off the walking lane.

## 3. Filled gaps and expanded the terrain

- Expanded the footprint to 19.6 × 17.5 m using additional Navcam coverage. Filled missing heights using the surrounding measured surface and blended missing texture from rover photo swatches.
- Made the render mesh and collision grid continuous across the footprint. Softened sharp height transitions where stereo wedges disagreed.
- Verified a 12.83 m walk through the expansion and a separate 9.22 m walk crossing inferred sections. Boundary collision and reset passed.

## 4. Doubled the footprint and refined the visuals

- Expanded to 28 × 24.5 m (686 m²). Of 30 evaluated Navcam inputs, 23 contributed. About 63% of the final footprint had stereo support. The route checker estimates 613 m² of walkable ground connected to spawn.
- Rejected stretched photo projections, prioritized sharper views, and blended grid-aligned seams. Sampled detail from rover photos in areas with weak coverage. Added a dusty horizon as visual context beyond the walkable boundary.
- Split the browser render into tiles so it can skip terrain outside the camera view. Kept the GLB as a standalone textured asset.
- Verified a 17.71 m walk past the slab and into the expansion and a separate 7.10 m walk across inferred terrain. Pointer lock, boundary collision, and reset passed in Chrome.
- The heightfield approximates steep occluded faces, and inferred surfaces can still look softer than measured rock. The slab is a recognizable local feature whose individual NASA name remains unverified.

## Implementation and file map

The release is self-contained for viewing after npm installs the locked dependencies. Source downloads are needed only to rebuild the terrain. The repository root also contains pre-existing project planning material. Run project commands inside `skinner-ridge/`.

| File | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `source/`, `sources.json`, `download.mjs` | Local archive cache, URL list, and reproducible downloader. The cache is ignored. OBJ, XML, and PNG inputs are checked against existing manifest hashes when available. |
| `reconstruct.mjs` | Checks PDS labels and motion counters, reads the stereo-derived OBJ positions and archived UVs, rasterizes overlapping wedges, rejects long source triangles and small disconnected fragments, and fuses agreeing heights with more weight for denser input. |
| `infill.mjs` | Solves missing heights across multiple grid scales with measured anchors. Adds small ripples where support is distant, limits abrupt stitch slopes, and smooths small ridges while retaining the layered slab. |
| `atlas.mjs`, `texture-infill.mjs` | Project archived Navcam textures using source UVs. Prefer well-resolved projections and penalize stretching and disagreement with the finished surface. Blend image edges, estimate missing base tone, and restore grain with overlapping measured photo swatches. |
| `polish-atlas.mjs` | Detects jumps aligned to regular mesh-grid lines in the embedded JPEG and feathers them over nearby texels. Repackages the GLB while preserving geometry and adjusts binary offsets. |
| `terrain.glb` | Standalone 36.8 MB textured export with 1,120,000 indexed triangles. The material uses baked rover lighting. The viewer's distant context is separate. |
| `ground.bin`, `ground.mjs` | Matching grid with little-endian Float32 heights followed by one byte per cell. Bits 0 and 1 identify the two terrain triangles. The current grid has both triangles in every cell. The sampler interpolates the same planes as the GLB and checks ground around a 16 cm player footprint. |
| `terrain.json`, `terrain-coverage.png` | Export statistics, input contributions, motion counters, XYZ references, hashes, and a map of measured anchors. Light tan marks full cell support, intermediate tan partial support, and dark brown inferred support. |
| `route-check.mjs`, `route.json` | Check the slab path and derive routes outside the previous crop and across inferred ground. A 14 cm sampling grid estimates the area connected to spawn. These routes are test fixtures and starting points for manual inspection. |
| `viewer.mjs`, `index.html` | Load the GLB, ground and route. Split the mesh into camera-culled tiles with shared position and UV buffers. Handle click-to-enter pointer lock, WASD, mouse look, axis-separated substeps, grounded movement, reset, and anisotropic filtering. |
| `landscape.mjs` | Adds estimated visual terrain past the playable perimeter and a dusty sky. Samples reflected edge textures near the perimeter and blends into more distant context. |
| `serve.mjs` | Serves the app on loopback only, with an allowlist for demo files and needed Three.js modules. |
| `capture.mjs`, `screenshots/` | Exercises Chrome through CDP with pointer lock and movement inputs, checks collision/reset/ground contact, and captures start, return, expansion, inferred ground and survey views. |

### Coordinate and texture conventions

Site 26 OBJ coordinates are north, east, down. The current origin is (77.49 m north, 2.51 m east, −14 m down). The export maps to west-up-north coordinates in metres. The grid is 801 × 701 vertices at 0.035 m spacing. The baked atlas is 5600 × 4900. Before post-processing, 16,648,135 texels had a usable photo projection and 10,791,865 needed inferred texture. The estimated surface uses 411,866 faces with incomplete stereo support out of 1,120,000. A stereo-supported triangle can still receive some texture or slope refinement.

### Commands and verification

```powershell
git clone https://github.com/piccsaso/nasa-hackathon.git
cd nasa-hackathon/skinner-ridge
npm.cmd ci
npm.cmd start
```

Open [the local viewer](http://127.0.0.1:8080/). Click **Enter terrain**. Steer just right of the slab, keep it on the left as you pass uphill, and turn back. WASD moves, mouse turns, R resets, and Esc releases the mouse. Desktop Chrome on Windows with Node.js 24 was the tested stack. The local viewer uses pinned Three.js 0.180.0, pngjs 7.0.0 and jpeg-js 0.4.4. The latter two packages are for build/capture processing.

To regenerate the assets, run `npm.cmd run download` followed by `npm.cmd run build`. Build runs reconstruction, atlas polishing and route verification in that order. Keep `terrain.glb`, `ground.bin`, `terrain.json`, `route.json`, the coverage map and screenshots aligned to the same build. Run `npm.cmd run capture` for the Chrome regression and new screenshots. To use a non-default Chrome install, pass its path after `npm.cmd run capture --`.

The final test walked 3.48 m past the slab and 14.23 m farther into the expansion. The edge stopped movement. After reset, a separate 7.10 m test stayed 1.65 m above the ground while crossing inferred cells. The route JSON holds planned distances, which differ from actual stopping positions in the browser. The exact published GLB and grid were also checked for valid GLB lengths, finite heights and all triangle-mask bits populated.

### Debugging history and tradeoffs

- Legacy PDS directories initially appeared to lack Sol 1709. The [Atlas search endpoint](https://pds-imaging.jpl.nasa.gov/api/search/atlas/_search) revealed Release 15. The exact successful mesh and related archive links are in [site selection](SITE_SELECTION.md). Common rover poses and overlap mattered more than the headline image count.
- The first build left cutouts off the path. The first feedback screenshot showed large flat brown polygons where the background showed through the mesh. This led to filling both geometry and texture rather than just hiding holes visually.
- A first expanded look exposed tall stereo stitch artifacts. Slope limiting and denoising reduced them. Terrain collision moved from single-triangle slope rejection to checks across the player footprint, which allowed ordinary small rubble while still blocking large rises and edges.
- A later feedback screenshot was a close downward view with blurred texture, hard tile edges, and faceted rock faces. Projection filtering, image-space feathering, and texture polishing addressed those artifacts. Reflected edge samples reduced streaks at the landscape perimeter.
- A runtime photo-detail shader offered little visible benefit and made software-rendered Chrome walking substantially slower. It was removed. Tile culling stayed, and the final atlas polish is baked into the GLB.
- The first headless Chrome attempt hit sandbox and crash-handler issues. The successful automation used local Chrome with SwiftShader and CDP user-gesture handling. A diagonal boundary test could slide along the edge by design, so it was changed to aim straight at the nearest edge.
- The archived source imagery, inferred fills, and decorative horizon are three distinct levels of evidence. The final result still has soft close detail on some steep and occluded surfaces. No face-by-face identification of the slab in the Mastcam-Z mosaic was verified.

### Initial project brief

> # Build a photorealistic, walkable patch of Mars from Perseverance imagery
>
> I’m building a NASA hackathon project. I want a small first-person level on the real surface of Mars. Think Minecraft-style freedom to move and look around, with realistic ground, rocks, slopes, and textures reconstructed from Perseverance observations. The demo should let me walk past one recognizable landmark, look back at it, and see a convincing 3D scene from both directions.
>
> Own the project end to end. Work through the phases below in sequence. If you have workspace, shell, and browser tools, use them to research, implement, run, and verify the build. Continue past the plan and site recommendation into the actual reconstruction. My workspace is on Windows. Inspect the current repo and available tools before choosing a stack. Preserve existing work and favor tools I can run locally without paid services.
>
> If you lack a necessary capability, make all the progress you can. Give me the exact missing file, command, or access needed to unblock the next step. Distinguish what you verified from what you proposed. Base any claim of a completed reconstruction on an actual exported asset and inspection.
>
> ## Phase 1 — Find the best-documented walkable landmark
>
> Search NASA and USGS sources and inspect actual archive records and images. Start with the Sol 1709 Jezero rim drive as one candidate. Compare it with a few other promising Perseverance sites, including nearby rover stops with repeat observations of a recognizable rock, outcrop, or ridge. Treat Drive 1004 as a reference for data formats and patch fusion. Its example viewer showed holes and stretched surfaces at close range, which are exactly the problems this build should avoid.
>
> Start with these sources:
> - Perseverance Raw Images Gallery: https://mars.nasa.gov/mars2020/multimedia/raw-images/
> - NASA PDS Navcam terrain mesh archive: https://planetarydata.jpl.nasa.gov/img/data/mars2020/mars2020_navcam_ops_mesh/
> - Mars 2020 camera data specification: https://pds-imaging.jpl.nasa.gov/documentation/Mars2020_Camera_Bundle_SIS.pdf
> - NASA’s Sol 1709 rim-drive reconstruction: https://science.nasa.gov/photojournal/video-perseverance-rovers-view-of-crater-rim-drive/
> - Drive 1004 sample data and processing notes: https://github.com/NASA-AMMOS/3DTilesSampleData/tree/master/m20-drive-1004
> - NASA Landform toolkit and reconstruction paper: https://github.com/NASA-AMMOS/Landform and https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18330
> - USGS Jezero terrain and orbital imagery: https://astrogeology.usgs.gov/search/map/mars-2020-jezero-crater-landing-site-products
>
> For each serious candidate, record the landmark, sol or site/drive identifiers, and how many usable stereo observations and distinct rover positions you can verify. Check for overlapping views of the landmark AND the nearby ground, including the side the player will see after walking past it. Check for calibrated camera products, usable pose or coordinate-frame metadata, archived XYZ/depth or terrain meshes, and sharp imagery suitable for texturing. Count distinct usable observations separately from thumbnails, alternate image versions, and repeated views. A shared sol alone does not establish a stereo pair.
>
> Rank candidates by how likely they are to yield one continuous, detailed first-person route. Favor verified coverage, alignment data, and texture quality over total image count. Report the evidence for the top candidates and choose one site yourself. Give the selected product IDs and verified download URLs. If Sol 1709 lacks accessible inputs, move to the best verified alternative.
>
> ## Phase 2 — Download the data and reconstruct the entire playable patch
>
> Define a compact footprint around the selected landmark. Aim for a short walk with room to pass the landmark and look back. Let the verified coverage determine the footprint. Reconstruct the full ground within that footprint, including the space between landmarks.
>
> Download a small representative batch first. Verify that the files open, the stereo products match their labels, and the geometry can be brought into a common coordinate system. Keep a manifest with product IDs, URLs, source credit, camera, sol, site/drive, and relevant calibration or pose metadata. Make downloads and processing repeatable.
>
> Use the strongest public data available. Where NASA provides stereo-derived XYZ or meshes for the selected observations, use them as measured geometry and align and fuse the patches yourself. Where you need to recover depth from images, use properly matched stereo pairs or overlapping calibrated views. Apply the archived camera models, rectify where appropriate, estimate depth, and reject unreliable matches. Preserve metric scale using verified calibration or metadata. Confirm coordinate frames and transforms before combining patches.
>
> Fuse the overlapping patches into a continuous textured mesh. Remove outliers, floating fragments, and long triangles that stretch across gaps. Preserve rock shapes and surface detail along the walk. Project the sharpest suitable rover imagery onto the geometry, accounting for occlusions, and blend seams and exposure differences. Check the result at standing height from multiple directions. Expand the batch only when extra observations improve coverage or texture quality.
>
> Use orbital elevation for broader terrain or distant context if helpful. If you interpolate small gaps, document where. Prefer shrinking or rerouting the playable area when a major gap has no trustworthy coverage. Keep reconstructed, orbital, and approximate regions identifiable in the project notes.
>
> Export a locally usable `terrain.glb` with textures, known units, a consistent up direction, and a suggested spawn point and facing. Include a separate collision mesh if it improves playability. Keep the original rover imagery and geometry traceable through the source manifest.
>
> ## Phase 3 — Make it walkable in a browser
>
> Use the existing frontend stack if suitable. Otherwise, use a simple local Three.js app. Load `terrain.glb`, start the player on verified ground at human eye height, and add click-to-enter mouse look, WASD, ground collision, and a reset control. Keep the camera grounded on slopes. Prevent the player from walking through the landmark or into holes, off steep edges, or beyond the usable area.
>
> Set up a clear starting view and a short route past the real landmark. Preserve the rover imagery’s visible detail at eye level. Keep the interface minimal, with controls and data credit easy to find.
>
> ## Phase 4 — Verify and deliver
>
> Run the app locally. Walk from spawn past the landmark, turn around, and look back from a second position. Check that the surface remains continuous, the landmark looks solid from both directions, nearby textures stay sharp, and the player cannot fall through the ground or clip through the landmark. Capture screenshots at standing height from both ends of the route. Fix the most visible problems and repeat the walk.
>
> Deliver the textured GLB, browser viewer, repeatable reconstruction scripts or documented commands, source manifest, and README. Include the exact commands to launch the demo, the selected location and why it won, the observations actually used, the approximate size of the playable patch, and any visible limitations. Report what you tested and what remains unverified.
>
> Begin with the site comparison and evidence of overlapping coverage. Then choose the best candidate and build the smallest complete version that meets the walk-past-and-look-back test.
